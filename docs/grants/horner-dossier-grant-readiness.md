## 1. Project Purpose

This file converts the Horner Dossier into a grant-readiness planning document for preservation, public-history, cemetery documentation, heritage interpretation, and future site-development work connected to Floyd Township, Johnson County, Arkansas.

The central historical subject is the August 2, 1864 killing of John Spencer Horner and the subsequent death of William Riley Horner on August 10, 1864 after being beaten during the same attack. The dossier frames the event within Civil War guerrilla violence, Union-aligned civilian targeting, family oral history, land records, military service records, tombstone inscriptions, and GPS site documentation.

---

## 2. Core Historical Case

The dossier identifies the Horner killing as a locally documented example of Civil War irregular violence in the Arkansas Ozarks. The case is unusually strong for public-history development because it combines multiple source types:

- 1942 Horner family oral-history transcript
- Tombstone inscriptions
- Federal land patent documentation, including Patent No. 19B / Choctaw scrip
- Census records
- Civil War compiled military service records
- Pension records
- Amnesty petition materials
- GPS site survey documentation
- Cemetery and descendant-line documentation

The dossier states that the Horner family was established on Sections 19 and 22 of Township 12 North, Range 24 West by the 1857 land patent, seven years before the August 1864 attack.

---

## 3. Grant-Ready Themes

### A. Civil War Civilian Violence in the Arkansas Ozarks

The project documents the localized, neighbor-against-neighbor nature of Civil War violence in Floyd Township. It is not framed as a battlefield project only. The stronger grant frame is civilian impact, irregular warfare, family displacement, and memory.

### B. Unionist Minority Experience in Johnson County

The dossier presents the Horner and Acord families as Union-affiliated households in a divided county. This allows the project to support educational programming about allegiance, coercion, retaliation, and survival in divided communities.

### C. Cemetery Preservation and Public Memory

Moore Cemetery and Yale Cemetery are key interpretive locations. William Riley Horner’s tombstone inscription — “by parties known to his family” — is central to the public-memory value of the project.

### D. Land, Removal, and Choctaw Scrip Context

The 1857 Choctaw scrip patent gives the project a deeper land-history frame. The dossier is careful not to treat the patent as proof of Horner family Native ancestry. It frames the patent as evidence of land acquisition through the post-removal Choctaw scrip system.

### E. Descendant-Led Local History

The dossier includes a direct descent line from John Spencer Horner through Elizabeth Horner Acord and John Samuel Acord to Daniel Bret Lingar, the compiler. This makes the project descendant-led rather than externally imposed.
